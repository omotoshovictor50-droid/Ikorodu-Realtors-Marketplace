# Legal checklist (reduce your risk)

I am an AI, not a lawyer, and nothing here is legal advice. These are the most common ways a property website gets into trouble. Have a Nigerian lawyer review them and the draft Privacy Policy, Terms of Use and Disclaimer **before** you share the site.

## 1. Photos, videos and logos (highest risk)
- Only publish photos and videos you **own or have written permission to use**.
- **The banner video:** the footage you supplied carries another creator's CapCut watermark ("Criadormercial"). I cropped the watermark out at your request, but that does not give you the right to use it, and hiding it can look worse if the owner complains. Get written permission, or replace it with your own or properly licensed footage. To replace it, change the files in `public/videos/` (`clip-1.mp4` to `clip-4.mp4` and their `.jpg` posters), rebuild, and re-upload `docs/`.
- Listing photos you take from other websites or from the realtor's WhatsApp need the owner's permission.
- Map data from OpenStreetMap is shown with its required credit. Keep that credit.

## 2. Only advertise what you are allowed to advertise
- Get **written permission** from the owner or authorised agent before listing any property. Keep the message or agreement.
- Remove a listing quickly when it is sold or the owner asks.
- Keep prices and availability up to date. Do not use words like "verified", "approved" or "trusted" for a property or realtor unless you really checked and can prove it. (Your logo tagline says "Trusted"; that is a claim you should be able to back up.)
- Never publish a property that does not exist or that you have not been authorised to advertise. The 100 sample listings are **not** in this final site.

## 3. Written agreements with realtors and owners
Use a short written agreement covering: permission to advertise, accuracy of the information, who handles the sale, the referral fee or commission, and that they, not you, are responsible for the property and title.

## 4. Personal information (Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023)
- You collect names, phone numbers and messages from enquiries. Keep them only as long as needed, keep them private, and delete them on request.
- Ask a lawyer whether you must register with the Nigeria Data Protection Commission or appoint a data protection contact.
- Use two-step verification on your Gmail and WhatsApp.

## 5. Business and licensing questions to ask a lawyer
- Register your business name with the CAC and show the registered name and number in the website footer once you have it.
- Whether the referral-fee model requires any estate-agent registration or licence in Lagos (for example with ESVARBON or Lagos State real estate regulators).
- Whether your Terms need extra clauses for payments, if you ever take any.

## 6. Protect buyers
The Disclaimer tells visitors never to pay before inspecting and checking documents with their own lawyer. Repeat that advice in your WhatsApp replies, and never ask anyone to pay you directly for a property unless a lawyer has set that up.
